## What it does

Ask a question about Spanish legislation — *"How long does the public administration have to answer a citizen?"* — and the assistant replies in plain language **and cites the exact article it used**, with a link back to the official source on boe.es. No made-up answers: if the law doesn't cover it, it says so instead of guessing.

The source is the **BOE** (Boletín Oficial del Estado), Spain's official gazette — a huge body of text that grows every single day.

## Why it's different

Most AI demos that "answer from documents" are impressive until you check them: they sound confident but can't prove where the answer came from, and nobody actually measures whether they're right.

This project takes the opposite approach — **measure first**:

- A fixed set of real legal questions, each with a known correct source, exists **before** any tuning.
- Every change has to beat the previous version on that test set, or it doesn't ship — the same idea as unit tests, but for answer quality.
- Every answer points to the specific article it's based on, so a human can verify it in seconds.

## How it works

  B --> C[Structure-aware chunks<br/>one per article]

  C --> E[(Hybrid index<br/>dense + BM25)]
  Q[Query] --> F[Hybrid retrieval<br/>RRF]
  E --> F

  G --> H[Grounded generation<br/>cite-or-refuse]
  H --> I[Cited answer]`} />

1. **Collect** the official documents from the BOE's open-data API.
2. **Split** them the smart way — one piece per legal article, keeping its place in the law (title → chapter → article) so each piece can cite itself precisely.
3. **Search** for the passages most relevant to your question, combining keyword matching (great for exact references like *"Article 14"*) with meaning-based search (great for paraphrases).
4. **Re-check** the top candidates with a more careful model that reads the question and each passage together, then keeps the best.
5. **Answer** using only those passages, adding the citations — or refusing if the answer isn't there.

## Results so far

On the test set, careful retrieval finds the right article for **every** question, and the generated answers stay faithful to the sources **~99% of the time** (they rarely invent anything). Each improvement — better search, smarter re-ranking — was kept only because the numbers said it helped.

And you don't have to take my word for it: the live demo includes a **quality-metrics page** that shows these scores directly, plus a **search-only mode** that lets you inspect exactly which articles the retriever returns before any answer is written.

## Built to resist misuse

A legal assistant that can be *tricked* into citing a law that doesn't exist is worse than no assistant at all. So it's also tested against people trying to break it — attempts to make it ignore its rules, leak its instructions, or invent citations. One of those tests caught a real problem: when pushed, the model would cite a source it never actually read. I added a check that catches and removes those fake citations before the answer ever reaches you — closing that specific weakness completely while leaving everything else working. The same "measure, find the flaw, fix it, prove it" loop used for accuracy, applied to safety.
